#+title: what's the point?
#+description: some reflections on the purpose of human existence
#+slug: insignificant
#+filetags: :personal:

This is not a coherent post, so don't expect a thesis or logical, sequenced
arguments. This is a flow of consciousness, so feel free to close the tab if you
don't like rambling.

I've been feeling more insignificant than usual lately. Like, seeing myself as
"a fleck of dust in the scope of our universe" type of insignificant. Of course,
I've always felt this way in one way or another. Growing up, it was the hope of
leaving a small town and /hoping/ to become more insignificant by becoming just
another one of the moving blurs in a large crowd, not significant enough to be
noticed. I always enjoyed being the observer rather than the observed.

But it's been different lately. Maybe it's just part of getting old, or maybe
it's the state of the world.

But there's this feeling not only of insignificance, but also hopelessness. Now,
those two might seem the same to some, but they're really not. You can be
insignificant in terms of the universe or your situation and still be a hopeful
person with goals, aspirations, and a positive outlook on life. Call it a
"realistic optimist" or something, if you want, but it exists and I would argue
that's what I used to be.

Now, imagine the same person walking along the timeline of life. As they walk
from one end to the other, that feeling of insignificance grows and consumes the
person more and more. It takes over places inside of them that used to contain
hope, aspirations, and dreams. Eventually, they're left as a nihilistic void of
their past self.

It may sound negative or depressing, but I don't think it is. It's just reality
and the acceptance of life as it exists. Buddhism explains that life is
suffering, but while that sounds like a neat explanation to all the negative
things in our world, I don't think it's a true statement. Life just /is/. It's
chaos, it's random, it's meaningless. We ascribe meaning to things around us
because that's what has driven human survival and evolution throughout the
millennia. But, it's all made up. Same as we make up language to communicate, or
words to describe colors without *really* knowing that we see the same colors as
other humans, it's all arbitrary.

But then again, who am I to tell others what has meaning? That's part of my
argument, I suppose. It's just that I, myself, have lost meaning. "You might be
depressed," you say. I'm aware; who isn't these days? But I was depressed and
felt true purpose for a long time, so it's not that easy of an answer. And I
don't even expect an answer, per se. It's just something I've noticed and can't
help but wonder what it all means. Perhaps I haven't lost meaning at all, I've
simply discovered that there is no meaning to any of this in the first place.

Some people will disagree and argue that you could go do =random thing= to feel
purpose. True, but to what end? If it's self-satisfaction, I don't think there's
much purpose there except to juice the brain with emotions that feel good.
That's certainly /a/ purpose, but of little value to me. Others might say you
could impact others besides yourself, or even make an impact where your name is
known. Also true, but for how long? Even the most famous people in history are
unknown or, once again, insignificant, on a long enough timeline. As we go back
in history, we find fewer and fewer names we care to write down or remember.
Some are written down just for the sake of recording them. But otherwise,
insignificant. In the scale of the universe, knowing humans a few thousand years
ago is just scratching the surface and the rest are lost to history.

Maybe being lost to history is the point. You arrive, you do whatever, and
you're gone in what seems like a flash of light. Returning yet again to being
just an observer.
